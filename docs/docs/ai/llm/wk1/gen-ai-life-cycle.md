:::warning Pre-train in LLM is the same as training in AI feild
Pre-training in the context of Large Language Models (LLMs) is essentially the same fundamental action as training in the broader AI field. Both involve updating model weights based on a dataset to minimize some loss function, but the terminology emphasizes different aspects of the process. In LLMs, "pre-training" is emphasized to distinguish this foundational training phase from "fine-tuning," which adapts the pre-trained model to specific tasks.
:::

#### Interaction with LLMs

:::info What is Inference?
Inference in the context of Large Language Models (LLMs) refers to the process of using a pre-trained and possibly fine-tuned model to generate predictions or responses based on new input data. During inference, the model applies the knowledge it acquired during training to produce outputs for given prompts. Unlike training, inference does not involve updating the model's weights; it solely focuses on utilizing the existing trained parameters to interpret and respond to inputs. This process can include tasks such as text generation, translation, summarization, or answering questions based on the prompt provided.
:::

- **Prompt Engineering**:
  - Interaction with LLMs is through natural language or human-written instructions, known as prompts.
  - The prompt is processed by the model to predict and generate text (inference).
  - The output generated by the model is called a completion.

#### Practical Applications
- **Use Cases and Applications**:
  - Adapting models for specific tasks through fine-tuning.
  - Deploying LLMs in applications to solve business and social tasks.
- **Interaction Paradigms**:
  - Different from traditional machine learning and programming, LLMs use natural language instructions.
  - Context window: The memory space available for the prompt, typically large enough for a few thousand words, varies by model.



------

:::info The bigger the model is, The better?
Large language models (LLMs) with more parameters are better suited to handle a wide variety of tasks and can provide accurate completions through zero-shot inference due to their increased representational power and better generalization capabilities. These models can capture and learn complex patterns and relationships within vast and diverse datasets, allowing them to understand and generate text across different domains effectively. Their ability to represent rich and diverse features of language enables them to perform tasks they weren't explicitly trained for, showcasing emergent properties such as zero-shot learning. 

However, when it comes to specific areas, smaller LLMs with fewer parameters can sometimes outperform larger models because they can be fine-tuned to overfit particular tasks, making them highly specialized and efficient for those tasks. Smaller models focus on a narrower set of features and patterns relevant to a specific task, resulting in better performance within that area. Additionally, smaller models require less computational power and memory, leading to faster training and inference times, making them more practical and resource-efficient for specialized applications. Thus, while larger models excel in generalization and versatility, smaller models can offer superior performance in specialized tasks due to their focused training and resource efficiency.
:::

## transformer

### Summary of the Transcript

#### Introduction to Transformer Architecture

- **Advancement Over RNNs**:
  - Transformer architecture significantly improved the performance of natural language tasks compared to Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs).
  - Led to a surge in generative capabilities.

#### Key Features of Transformer Architecture

- **Self-Attention Mechanism**:
  - Ability to learn the relevance and context of all words in a sentence.
  - Applies attention weights to relationships between words, regardless of their position in the input.
  - Enhances the model's capability to understand complex tasks and contextual dependencies.

- **Attention Map**:
  - Illustrates the attention weights between each word and every other word in a sentence.
  - Example: The word "book" is strongly connected to "teacher" and "student".

#### How Transformer Models Work

- **Architecture Overview**:
  - Split into two parts: Encoder and Decoder.
  - Both parts share similarities and work together.
  - Based on the original "Attention is All You Need" paper.

- **Tokenization**:
  - Converts words into numbers (token IDs) to process text.
  - Token IDs can represent whole words or parts of words.
  - The same tokenizer must be used for training and text generation.

- **Embedding Layer**:
  - Transforms token IDs into vectors in a high-dimensional space.
  - Encodes the meaning and context of tokens.
  - Example: Previous models like Word2Vec also used vector embedding spaces.

- **Positional Encoding**:
  - Adds information about the word order to the input tokens.
  - Preserves the relevance of the word position in the sentence.

- **Self-Attention Layer**:
  - Analyzes relationships between tokens in the input sequence.
  - Learns attention weights reflecting the importance of each word to others.

- **Multi-Headed Self-Attention**:
  - Multiple sets of self-attention weights (heads) are learned independently.
  - Each head captures different aspects of language (e.g., entity relationships, activities, properties like rhyming).

#### Processing and Output

- **Fully-Connected Feed-Forward Network**:
  - Processes the output from the self-attention layer.
  - Produces a vector of logits proportional to probability scores for each token.

- **Softmax Layer**:
  - Normalizes logits into probability scores for each word in the vocabulary.
  - Identifies the most likely predicted token based on the highest score.

### Key Takeaways

- **Transformer Advantages**:
  - Improved performance over RNNs in natural language tasks.
  - Enhanced contextual understanding through self-attention mechanisms.

- **Self-Attention**:
  - Central to the transformer’s ability to process language effectively.
  - Enables the model to capture complex dependencies and relationships between words.

- **Multi-Headed Attention**:
  - Allows the model to learn various language aspects, improving overall understanding.

- **Tokenization and Embeddings**:
  - Essential steps in preparing text for model processing.
  - Ensure consistent representation of input and output.

This summary covers the essential aspects of the transformer architecture and its advantages in natural language processing tasks. If you have any follow-up questions or need further clarification, feel free to ask!



### Detailed Prediction Process in Transformer Architecture

- **Overview**:
  - Explanation of how the transformer architecture works end-to-end using a sequence-to-sequence translation task.
  - Example task: Translating the French phrase *"J'adore l'apprentissage automatique"* to English.

#### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Tokenization**:
   - Input words are tokenized using the same tokenizer that trained the network.
   - Tokens are fed into the encoder side of the network.

2. **Encoder Processing**:
   - Tokens pass through the embedding layer.
   - Tokens are processed by multi-headed attention layers.
   - Outputs go through a feed-forward network, producing a deep representation of the input sequence’s structure and meaning.

3. **Decoder Processing**:
   - The representation from the encoder influences the decoder's self-attention mechanisms.
   - A start-of-sequence token triggers the decoder to predict the next token.
   - Decoder outputs pass through self-attention layers, feed-forward network, and softmax layer to produce the first token.

4. **Token Generation Loop**:
   - The output token is fed back into the decoder input to predict the next token.
   - This loop continues until an end-of-sequence token is predicted.
   - Final sequence of tokens is detokenized into words to produce the output.

#### Variations of Transformer Models

- **Encoder-Only Models**:
  - Used for tasks where input and output sequences are the same length.
  - Can be adapted for classification tasks (e.g., sentiment analysis).
  - Example: BERT.

- **Encoder-Decoder Models**:
  - Perform well on sequence-to-sequence tasks like translation.
  - Can be scaled for general text generation tasks.
  - Examples: BART, T5.

- **Decoder-Only Models**:
  - Commonly used and capable of generalizing to most tasks.
  - Examples: GPT family, BLOOM, Jurassic, LLaMA.

#### Key Concepts

- **Attention Mechanism**:
  - Essential for understanding the context and relevance of each word in the input.
  
- **Self-Attention**:
  - Multiple heads learn different aspects of language simultaneously.

- **Embedding Layer**:
  - Converts tokens into high-dimensional vectors, capturing meaning and context.

- **Positional Encoding**:
  - Adds word order information to maintain relevance within the sequence.

- **Softmax Layer**:
  - Normalizes logits into probability scores to determine the most likely next token.

#### Practical Use and Prompt Engineering

- **Interaction with Models**:
  - Users interact with transformer models through natural language prompts.
  - Prompt engineering involves creating written prompts to instruct the model.
  - Understanding the underlying architecture aids in reading model documentation and effective prompt creation.


## Prompting and prompt engineering

### Key Terminology Recap

- **Prompt**: The text input fed into the model.
- **Inference**: The process of generating text.
- **Completion**: The output text generated by the model.
- **Context Window**: The amount of text/memory available for the prompt.

### Prompt Engineering

- **Prompt Engineering**: The process of refining the prompt to achieve desired model behavior.
- **In-Context Learning**: Including examples of the task within the prompt to guide the model.

### In-Context Learning Examples

1. **Zero-Shot Inference**:
   - Providing only the task instruction and input data.
   - Effective with large models but can be challenging for smaller models.
   - Example: Asking a model to classify the sentiment of a review without providing examples.

2. **One-Shot Inference**:
   - Including one example of the task within the prompt.
   - Helps smaller models understand the task better.
   - Example: Providing a sample review and its sentiment classification before the actual input review.

3. **Few-Shot Inference**:
   - Including multiple examples of the task within the prompt.
   - Useful for smaller models to grasp the task effectively.
   - Example: Providing a mix of positive and negative reviews with their classifications.

### Model Performance and Size

- **Large Models**:
  - Good at zero-shot inference.
  - Capable of performing multiple tasks well due to their extensive training and large parameter size.

- **Smaller Models**:
  - Benefit from one-shot or few-shot inference.
  - Generally good at tasks similar to those they were specifically trained on.

### Context Window Limitation

- **Limitations**:
  - The context window size restricts the amount of in-context learning.
  - If performance doesn't improve with multiple examples, fine-tuning the model might be necessary.

### Fine-Tuning

- **Fine-Tuning**: 
  - Additional training using new data to make the model more capable of specific tasks.
  - Will be explored in detail in Week 2 of the course.

### Configuration Settings

- **Experimentation**:
  - Once a suitable model is found, various settings can be adjusted to influence the style and structure of the generated completions.
  - Further details on these settings will be covered in the next video.

### Key Takeaways

- **Effective Prompt Engineering**:
  - Crucial for guiding model behavior, especially with smaller models.
  - Involves techniques like zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot inference.
- **Model Selection**:
  - Larger models are versatile but resource-intensive.
  - Smaller models may require more prompt engineering and fine-tuning.




## Generative configuration

### Configuration Parameters for Next-Word Generation

1. **Introduction**:
   - Configuration parameters are used during inference to control the model’s behavior and output.
   - These parameters differ from training parameters and influence aspects like the number of tokens and creativity of the output.

2. **Max New Tokens**:
   - Limits the number of tokens the model will generate.
   - Acts as a cap on the number of iterations for token selection, though other stop conditions like the end-of-sequence token can also terminate generation.

3. **Greedy Decoding**:
   - The model selects the word with the highest probability each time.
   - Works well for short generations but can lead to repetitive sequences.

4. **Random Sampling**:
   - Introduces variability by selecting words based on their probability distribution.
   - Helps reduce repetition but may lead to outputs that wander off-topic or are nonsensical.
   - Requires explicit enabling in some implementations, like Hugging Face's `do_sample=True`.

5. **Top-K Sampling**:
   - Limits selection to the top K tokens with the highest probabilities.
   - Provides controlled randomness by restricting choices to a smaller set of likely words.

6. **Top-P Sampling (Nucleus Sampling)**:
   - Limits selection to tokens whose cumulative probabilities add up to a specified threshold (p).
   - Ensures sensible outputs while allowing some variability.

7. **Temperature**:
   - Adjusts the randomness of the output by scaling the probability distribution.
   - **Low Temperature (\<1)**: Concentrates probabilities on fewer words, leading to predictable text.
   - **High Temperature (\>1)**: Spreads probabilities more evenly, increasing randomness and creativity.
   - **Default Temperature (=1)**: Uses the unaltered probability distribution.

### Practical Implications

- **Max New Tokens**: Controls the length of the generated text.
- **Greedy Decoding**: Ensures the most probable word is selected, but can cause repetition.
- **Random Sampling**: Adds variability but may require constraints to maintain coherence.
- **Top-K and Top-P Sampling**: Provide a balance between randomness and control, ensuring more natural and sensible text generation.
- **Temperature**: Fine-tunes the level of creativity and randomness in the output.

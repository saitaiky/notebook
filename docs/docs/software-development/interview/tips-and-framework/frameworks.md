## Broad Then Deep Framework

The "Broad Then Deep" framework is a versatile approach for structuring responses to interview questions. It involves first listing multiple options (broad) and then selecting one to discuss in detail (deep). This method helps candidates present a well-rounded analysis and demonstrate thorough decision-making skills.

### Overview of the Framework

- **Broad Step:**
  - Start by listing several options or possibilities related to the question.
  - Provide context and relevant information about each option.
- **Deep Step:**
  - Choose one option from the list.
  - Discuss this option in detail, including reasons for choosing it, pros and cons, and trade-offs.

### Example

- **Example Question:** "Which Google product do you think will be the most successful in 10 years?"
- **Broad:**
  - Enumerate potential candidates: YouTube, Google Search, Google Photos, Android.
  - Provide context: Discuss why each might have long-term potential.
  
- **Deep:**
  - Select one option: For instance, Google Search.
  - Explain the choice: Competitive advantages, market dominance, integration with voice assistants, and smart home devices.
  - Analyze: Highlight reasons, potential impacts, and why it stands out compared to other options.

### Tips for Using the Framework

- **Don't Overapply Frameworks:** Adapt the framework to fit the specific question rather than rigidly following a method.
- **Authenticity:** Be genuine in your analysis and reasoning.
- **Context:** Give context around each option in the broad step to make your reasoning clear.
- **Depth:** Dive deeply into the chosen option to show comprehensive understanding and critical thinking.

## The 3 Point Method

The "3 Point Method" is a structured approach to delivering clear and concise responses during interviews. This method helps keep the interviewer engaged and ensures your key points are communicated effectively, especially if the interviewer is distracted or has had a long day.

### Understand the Interviewer’s Perspective**

- **Context:** Interviewers may be tired or preoccupied with other tasks, making it essential to present your ideas clearly and succinctly.
- **Clarity:** Your goal is to make it easy for the interviewer to follow your points, even if they aren't fully attentive.

### The 3 Point Method Structure

Question: "What’s your favorite product and why?"

1. **Tell Them What You’ll Say:**
   - **Introduction:** Briefly introduce the main points you will cover.
   - **Example:** "Snapchat is my favorite product because of three main reasons: its ephemerality, its disintegration from other apps, and its treatment of privacy."
2. **Say It:**
   - **Details:** Elaborate on each point with specific details and examples.
   - **Example:**
     - **Ephemerality:** "Snapchat's ephemeral nature keeps interactions light and fun, unlike other messaging apps that promote work-related planning."
     - **Disintegration:** "Snapchat doesn’t integrate with other apps, allowing for a creative and separate space compared to Facebook’s interconnectedness."
     - **Privacy:** "Snapchat prioritizes user privacy through its design, avoiding the trust issues associated with Facebook."
3. **Tell Them What You Just Said:**
   - **Summary:** Recap the main points to reinforce your message.
   - **Example:** "In summary, Snapchat is my favorite product due to its ephemeral messaging, separation from other apps, and strong privacy focus."

### Practical Application

- **Consistency:** Use this method for various questions to maintain a clear and organized response.
- **Engagement:** Helps keep the interviewer engaged by providing a structured and easy-to-follow answer.
- **Flexibility:** Applicable to both small and large questions, adding a backbone to your responses.

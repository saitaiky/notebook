# Building Your Solutions Architect Story Bank

Behavioral questions in interviews assess your core competencies, such as communication, team orientation, and cultural fit. A well-prepared story bank will help you showcase these attributes effectively. Below are the steps to build your story bank.

## 1. Research Your Target Company

Understanding the culture of your target company is crucial. Here’s how to gather relevant insights:

- **Company Values:** Identify the core values of the company. For example:
  - **Google:** Building belonging, Expanding opportunity, Protecting users, Responding to crises, Advancing sustainability.
  - **Airbnb:** Champion the mission, Be a host, Embrace the adventure, Be a cereal entrepreneur.
- **Product Value Propositions:** Examine how the company presents its products, particularly cloud solutions, to customers. This reflects company priorities and values.
- **Social Media:** Observe the company's branding and voice on platforms like LinkedIn or Twitter. This helps gauge the company’s tone (e.g., playful, formal).
- **Company Review Sites:** Check reviews on Glassdoor and LinkedIn to understand employee perspectives and company culture.
- **Annual Reports:** Review annual reports or 10-K filings from the investor relations page for insights into company strategy and values.
- **Engineering Blogs:** Explore the engineering blog to understand the company’s technical culture and approach to problem-solving.

## 2. Choose Your Stories

Select 5-10 stories that reflect both your technical and interpersonal skills. Follow these guidelines:

- **Relevance:** Ensure each story aligns with at least one company value and involves recent experiences (within the last two years).
- **Diversity:** Include a mix of successful projects, challenges faced, and lessons learned. Highlight both positive outcomes and situations where things didn’t go as planned.
- **Detail and Nuance:** Choose stories with enough depth to discuss at length. Avoid overly simplistic stories that can be summarized in a sentence.

Here are a few common questions where the story bank comes in handy. Use these as inspiration if you're having trouble choosing.
- "Tell me about the project you're most proud of."
- "What's the most complex project you've worked on?"

:::info Do's and Don'ts
- **Do:** Choose recent stories.
- **Don't:** Select stories lacking details.
- **Do:** Map stories to company values.
- **Don't:** Pick stories you're uncomfortable discussing.
- **Do:** Include a range of experiences (successes, failures, lessons).
:::

## 3. Record the Details

Document each story thoroughly, including both technical and interpersonal aspects:

- **Context:** Describe the project or task, including its significance to the business.
- **Actions:** Detail your role, decisions made, and steps taken.
- **People Involvement:** Note interactions with team members, customers, or stakeholders.
- **Challenges:** Include any difficulties faced and how they were addressed.
- **Outcomes:** Explain the results and any impact on the company or team.

**Structure to Follow:**
- **Who:** Key individuals involved.
- **What:** The task or project at hand.
- **When:** Timeline or key milestones.
- **Where:** The setting or context.
- **Why:** Purpose and goals of the project.

:::info Do's and Don'ts
- **Do:** Consider all stakeholders, including internal team members and customers.
- **Don't:** Overlook any interpersonal dynamics or conflicts.
- **Do:** Connect your actions to company values.
:::

## 4. Practice Your Stories

Once your story bank is complete, practice delivering your stories:

- **Use Frameworks:** Apply structured frameworks (e.g., STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result) to organize your responses.
- **Focus on Key Points:** Keep your answers concise and focused, highlighting the most relevant aspects.
- **Simulate Interviews:** Practice with mock interviews or with peers to refine your delivery and receive feedback.

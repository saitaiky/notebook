While the interview experts don't recommend heavy use of frameworks, the STAR framework can be a helpful starting point for structuring behavioral interview answers, especially where rambling is a real danger.

## What is STAR?

**STAR** stands for:

- **Situation**:
    - What relevant context does the interviewer need to understand the challenge?
    - Clearly define the context and background of the situation. Mention specific roles, teams, and the environment to set the stage.
- **Task**:
    - What was your specific role in this situation?
    - Identify the key challenges or objectives. Clearly articulate what needed to be achieved or resolved.
- **Action**:
    - What steps did you take to solve the problem?
    - Detail the steps you took to address the task. Explain your research, planning, and execution. Include any collaboration with other teams or stakeholders.
- **Result**:
    - What was the outcome? (use numbers if you can!)
    - Highlight the outcomes and impact of your actions. Use specific metrics to quantify success and link the results back to the initial task.

The purpose of STAR is to keep you focused. If implemented correctly, you'll communicate all the relevant context needed to answer the question while highlighting your specific actions and achievements in a concise way.

Interviewers want to see:

-   Strong listening skills. Can you identify the right information, seek it out, and listen without bias?
-   Adaptive communication. Do you "know your audience"? Do you tailor your message as needed?
-   Logic, clarity, honesty, and respect. Can you construct a logical argument and communicate it clearly? Are you honest, and respectful to all of those around you? Even those who disagree?


:::warning Tips for Using the STAR Framework
- **Avoid Over-Rehearsing**: **Authenticity** is key in behavioral interviews. Rehearse high-level examples and adapt them to your interviewer in a natural tone.
- **Prepare for Follow-Ups**: Be ready for follow-up questions by understanding your examples thoroughly.
- **Stay Focused**: Use STAR to stay concise and relevant without rambling.
:::

## A Time When You Disagreed with a Colleague?

The lesson provides a structured approach to answering behavioral interview questions using the STAR framework. It emphasizes the importance of setting the context (Situation), clarifying the specific task (Task), detailing the actions taken (Action), and highlighting the outcomes (Result). The example used demonstrates how a lead engineer handled a complex situation involving conflicting priorities between product management and upper management, ultimately finding a balanced solution that met everyone's needs.

### Situation

In my previous role as a Technical Account Manager (TAM) for the open API business at a FAANG company, we were exposing APIs to third-party developers globally. These developers often communicated issues and suggestions via support tickets. One day, an external developer shared his API issue on LinkedIn, seeking advice from fellow developers. This incident highlighted the inefficiency of our current support system and the need for a more collaborative platform.

### Task

I identified two primary issues: the traditional support system's lack of scalability and slow response times, and the absence of a collaborative platform for developers. My task was to propose and implement a solution that would address these problems, improve developer support, and enhance collaboration among developers.

### Action

To tackle these issues, I proposed creating a developer community. I conducted research, gathered customer feedback, and documented the benefits of such a community. I highlighted that this community would enhance API reach, leverage developer expertise, reduce support costs, and improve product development. Addressing leadership concerns, I compared different platform options and suggested GitHub due to its cost-effectiveness and accessibility. I also proposed a "community moderator" role to ensure effective moderation.

### Result

The implementation of the developer community on GitHub led to significant success. Over three months, more than 1000 threads were created, with 75% of interactions led by external developers. The community also generated over 100 articles, including blogs and new API implementations. Importantly, there was a 30% reduction in support tickets, demonstrating improved support efficiency and developer engagement.


## A Time You Convinced Others to Support Your Idea?

The example involves a Technical Account Manager (TAM) who noticed an external developer discussing an API issue on LinkedIn. Recognizing the inefficiency of the current support system and the lack of a collaborative platform, the TAM proposed creating a developer community. This community would enhance support, reduce costs, and foster knowledge sharing. The TAM addressed leadership concerns, chose GitHub for implementation, and saw significant positive outcomes, including reduced support tickets and active developer engagement.

### How to preparing

FAANG companies highly value innovation, experimentation, and the ability to fail fast. Here’s how to prepare a strong proposal to overcome resistance and garner support for your initiative:

- **Executive Summary**: Provide an overview of what you aim to achieve.
- **Current State**: Describe the existing process or situation.
- **Customer Definition**: Identify who the customer is and what problem or opportunity they face.
- **Customer Benefit**: Explain how the initiative will serve the customer.
- **Validation/Evidence**: Provide evidence for the need to solve this problem, often referred to as the "voice of the customer" (VOC).
- **Vision for Success**: Define what success looks like and outline the entire customer journey.
- **Example Solutions**: Present multiple potential solutions, as this is useful in early stages.
- **Data Points**: Plan and justify each stage of the proposal with relevant data.
- **Success Metrics**: Specify how you will measure progress and success.
-. **Cost and Timelines**: Outline the associated costs and timelines.

#### Steps to Follow

1. **Write Down Your Proposal**: Clarify your thoughts and create a shareable document.
2. **Gather Feedback**: Share the proposal with trusted colleagues or your boss to refine it.
3. **Present to Stakeholders**: Address feedback and seek their endorsement.

:::info Use VOC as a foundation when you work for a customer-centric company.
In customer-centric companies, the "voice of the customer" (VOC) is crucial for decision-making and justifying the need for change.
:::

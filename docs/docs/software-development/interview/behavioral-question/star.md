While the interview experts don't recommend heavy use of frameworks, the STAR framework can be a helpful starting point for structuring behavioral interview answers, especially where rambling is a real danger.

## What is STAR?

**STAR** stands for:

- **Situation:** What relevant context does the interviewer need to understand the challenge?
- **Task:** What was your specific role in this situation?
- **Action:** What steps did you take to solve the problem?
- **Result:** What was the outcome? (use numbers if you can!)

The purpose of STAR is to keep you focused. If implemented correctly, you'll communicate all the relevant context needed to answer the question while highlighting your specific actions and achievements in a concise way.

## Example: Tell Me About a Time When You Disagreed with a Colleague

### Situation
First, think back to your story bank. Choose a story that stands out as the most challenging and nuanced, and map it to one or more company values. Describe the context of your story to set the stage and explain why this problem is worth discussing. If you can tie the context back to company goals or business priorities, that's a bonus!

> **Question:** Tell me about a time when you disagreed with a coworker.
> 
> **Answer:** In my last role, I had just switched to a new team where I worked as lead engineer. My first project was orchestrating a major feature release. Product management wanted to launch three new features for testing to validate the product roadmap before raising a Series B round from investors. However, we'd just dealt with a massive setback in our coding progress because of previous work done sloppily, requiring our team to redesign much of our system architecture from the ground up. This cost us significant engineering time and put everyone under a lot of stress. I wanted to hold off on the feature release until we could implement a few infrastructure changes that would allow us to more easily test in the future.

### Task
If your situation already described the task, you can skip this step. Otherwise, clarify your specific role and the goals you were meant to accomplish.

> **Answer:** The company goal was to solidify the product roadmap and be investor-ready within just one month. Launching the feature set for testing was a major part of meeting this goal. As lead engineer, I was tasked with implementing long-term solutions, not quick fixes. I was at a crossroads between upper management pushing for a full feature release and my commitment to quality engineering.

### Action
Describe how you tackled the problem, highlighting relevant skills and company values. Include collaboration and communication with your team and other stakeholders.

> **Answer:** I asked if the one-month deadline was set in stone and was assured that it was. I made a list of options and shared them with my manager. I proposed three choices: delay the release to implement changes, launch a reduced feature set while investing in infrastructure, or launch the full set and delay updates. I realized launching an altered feature set might be possible, so I met with the product manager and a UX researcher to discuss validating the hypothesis with a reduced launch. I shared my plan for infrastructure updates to facilitate easier testing. The product manager appreciated the approach, and we got feedback from his manager and the CEO, who prioritized a solid roadmap over specific feature tests.

### Result
Describe how the situation was resolved and any lessons learned. Use specific outcomes to show the impact of your actions.

> **Answer:** We decided on a reduced launch while completing the system design changes. The reduced launch went smoothly, the product team validated its hypothesis, and we completed backend work without further issues. This experience taught me to check assumptions before changing direction and to align everyone’s goals effectively.

## Tips for Using the STAR Framework

- **Avoid Over-Rehearsing:** Authenticity is key in behavioral interviews. Rehearse high-level examples and adapt them to your interviewer in a natural tone.
- **Prepare for Follow-Ups:** Be ready for follow-up questions by understanding your examples thoroughly.
- **Stay Focused:** Use STAR to stay concise and relevant without rambling.

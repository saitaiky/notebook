## Know Your Audience

The key to effective success as a SA will often hinge on your ability to tailor your message to your audience.

- **Presenting to Executives**:
    - Cut the technical speak.
    - Begin by presenting your solution before explaining *why*.
    - Focus on benefits, not features.
- **Presenting to Technical Audiences**:
    - Be prepared to talk details.
    - Avoid "marketing lingo".
    - Start with the problem statement and build up to your solution with evidence.
- **Collecting Customer Requirements**:
    - Gauge the technical knowledge and communication style of your audience before you begin.
    - Serve the customer by being tactful, respectful, upbeat, and responsive to customer cues.
    - Adjust your strategy or communication style if things aren't going well.

## Project Authority When Presenting

To instill confidence in your solution, you need to project confidence. Here are some nonverbal communication tricks to develop "executive presence":

- Take up plenty of space. Avoid hunching or slouching.
- Calm your breathing. Slow, deep breaths put your body at ease.
- Make eye contact. Focus on the point between someone's eyes if direct eye contact is uncomfortable.
- Use gestures sparingly to punctuate speech and draw attention to key points. Open hand gestures convey friendliness.
- Be aware of your movements and facial expressions. Minimize fidgeting to avoid conveying nervousness.

When speaking:

- Vary your tone and tempo.
- Pause before major points and take a moment to collect your thoughts before answering questions.
- Avoid speaking too fast.
- Repeat questions back before answering, but don't overdo it.

## Active Listening During Discovery

During discovery calls, aim to come off as friendly and less authoritative.

- **Show Appreciation**:
    - Acknowledge your customers' time and intelligence.
    - Show that you need something from them.
- **Active Listening Techniques**:
    - Smile.
    - Lean forward to signal engagement and interest.
    - Minimize fidgeting to avoid appearing rude.
    - Avoid looking at the clock or a watch too often to prevent seeming distracted.

## Anticipate "Gotchas"

Presentations can sometimes erupt into unexpected arguments. Stay calm and redirect the group's attention back to you. This shows your ability to handle objection handling and conflict resolution, which are essential parts of the job.

- **Common "Gotchas"**:
    - Questions you can't answer.
    - Obstinate objections to your solution.
    - Surprising responses meant to throw you off balance.

Remember the framework for handling objections covered in the last lesson for a refresher.
